## Business model

Hybrid revenue: subscription (50.6% of revenue, H1 2023) and volume-based (49.0%), plus a small services line (0.4%). Independently, revenue also splits by solution category: **provider solutions** (~70% of total, tools that help providers manage payer workflows) and **patient payment solutions** (~30%, tools for collecting from patients directly). These are two overlapping classifications of the same revenue pool, not contradictory figures — provider solutions shows up inside both subscription and volume-based revenue.

## Scale

- ~30,000 clients, ~1M distinct providers, including 18 of the top 22 US News Best Hospitals
- 2022: over 4 billion healthcare payment transactions, over $900B in gross claims volume, ~50% of US patients covered
- TAM: ~$15B today, growing to ~$20B by 2027 (5% CAGR, company estimate)
- Current penetration: ~3% of hospital segment, ~7% of ambulatory segment
- 82% win rate against competitors over the trailing 30 months

## Growth and retention

- Revenue growth: 22% (2021→2022); decelerated to 12.3% (H1 2023 vs H1 2022) — worth investigating whether this is seasonal, macro, or structural deceleration
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR): 109.5% (FY2022), 109.7% (TTM June 2023)
- **GRR not disclosed** — a genuine diligence gap. NRR alone cannot distinguish a low-churn/low-expansion business from a high-churn/high-expansion business netting to the same headline number. This is a data-room question a real PE team would put directly to management, not something to infer from its absence.
- Top 10 clients = only 11.4–11.7% of revenue — low customer concentration risk

## Profitability

- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 44.0% (2021) → 41.9% (2022) → 43.0% (H1 2023) — dipped before recovering; worth checking whether the dip ties to acquisition integration costs ($10.2M in 2021 vs $2.2M in 2022) or reflects real margin pressure
- Net loss margin improving: (8.1)% (2021) → (6.2)% (2022) → (5.5)% (H1 2023)
- Key addbacks to reach Adjusted EBITDA: interest expense, income tax, D&A, stock-based comp, acquisition/integration costs, asset and lease impairments, cost of extinguishing debt, IPO-related costs

## Acquisition history (buy-and-build platform strategy)

Formed 2017 via Bain Capital/EQT-sponsored merger of Navicure and ZirMed. Eight acquisitions since 2018, each filling a specific gap in the pre-service → service → post-service healthcare payments workflow rather than random bolt-ons:

- **Recondo Technology** — AI/RPA patient access and prior-auth automation (pre-service)
- **Connance** — predictive analytics for collections prioritization
- **PARO Decision Support** — presumptive charity scoring
- **UPMC's Ovation** — claims monitoring
- **eSolutions** — Medicare-specific RCM, 6,000+ payer connections; $1.3B valuation (Aug 2020); the single largest deal, creating the first platform spanning both commercial and government payers
- **Patientco** — omnichannel patient payments (post-service)
- **HealthPay24** — patient payment platform (from EngageSmart)
- Practice Insight — acquired by eSolutions prior to Waystar's acquisition of eSolutions

Individual deal financials are not publicly disclosed except eSolutions (SEC "significant acquisition" threshold likely triggered the disclosure requirement there specifically).

## Risk factors

- Revenue growth deceleration (22% → 12.3%) needs monitoring — flag as a threshold thesis-breaker if it continues
- GRR opacity — true churn risk cannot be independently verified from public filings
- Competitive risk from EHR vendors (Epic, Oracle Cerner) building native competing functionality
- CMS/reimbursement policy risk — indirect but real, since provider spend on vendors is downstream of provider reimbursement health
- High leverage (~7.6x pre-IPO) means EBITDA sensitivity to first-lien covenant compliance is a live risk under a growth-deceleration scenario

## Thesis fit

Core proof-of-concept for the RCM sub-sector thesis: recurring, workflow-embedded revenue; low customer concentration; genuine buy-and-build platform economics; and a clean PE value-creation case study (EQT's delever-before-IPO execution). Primary open question is whether the H1 2023 growth deceleration is transient or structural — worth revisiting once FY2023 full-year and FY2024 numbers are available.
