## AI maturity

High. RCM is the most PE-mature segment of AI in healthcare — proven ROI, real revenue at scale, multiple completed take-private and recapitalization transactions.

## Key players

- **Waystar** — hybrid subscription + volume-based RCM platform; ~30,000 clients, ~1M providers; NRR 109.7% (TTM June 2023); IPO'd June 2024 (EQT-backed)
- **R1 RCM** — end-to-end revenue cycle outsourcing + autonomous coding AI; ~$2.1B revenue; taken private by TowerBrook and CD&R in an $8.9B deal (Nov 2024)
- **Cotiviti** — payment accuracy and data analytics for payers; $11B recapitalization co-sponsored by Veritas Capital and KKR
- **Optum (UnitedHealth)** — integrated payer-provider RCM at scale
- **Infinx** — India-based AI-driven RCM serving the US market; KKR-backed minority stake

## PE attractiveness

Very high. ROI is directly measurable in dollars saved (denial reduction, days-in-AR improvement, cost-to-collect reduction) rather than softer clinical-outcome metrics. Deep EHR integration creates genuine switching costs. This is the segment with the most completed PE transactions to date.

## PE firms present, stakes, and strategic vision

- **TowerBrook Capital Partners + Clayton, Dubilier & Rice** — acquired R1 RCM in an $8.9B take-private (Nov 2024), the largest sponsor-driven health-tech public-to-private deal on record. Post-close, partnered with Palantir on an AI initiative targeting coding, billing, and denial management — the strategic vision is an autonomous revenue cycle with minimal human touch on routine claims.
- **Veritas Capital + KKR** — co-sponsored an $11B recapitalization of Cotiviti, doubling down on payer-side data analytics and payment accuracy.
- **KKR** — separately holds a significant minority stake in Infinx, extending the RCM thesis into an India-based delivery model serving US health systems.
- **EQT** — built Waystar from the 2017 Navicure/ZirMed merger through 8 acquisitions (2018–2023) into a platform IPO'd in June 2024; used IPO proceeds to retire the more expensive second-lien debt tranche first, a classic delever-before-exit move.
